What we learned, and what is next

Lessons

The evaluation set changed the product more than any feature idea. Start it in week one. Eight of our 32 tools exist because a question failed.

Prompt instructions are not rules. Everything that must always hold (ask the window question, never sum stages, confirm before send) moved into code and tests.

Honest numbers are cheaper than inflated ones. The 27% blind result was uncomfortable and produced seven fixes and 32 scenarios in two days.

Deploy early. CORS, the business clock and the ledger’s durability only appeared once the system left a laptop.

Next

The live second pass on fresh questions, per category, and planner agreement across repeated runs.

Longer conversations: “chase the oldest one” after a list, “tell me more about the second decision” after a briefing, “do it” after an expedite check.

One “normal” rule everywhere; alert usefulness measured with a real manager over two weeks.

Real data: stage-entry dates and defect records would give the lead-time model covariates and the alerts a quality signal.
